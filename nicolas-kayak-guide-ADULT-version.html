A pedal kayak is a boat you drive with your FEET — like a bike on the water. There's still a paddle, but the pedal drive does most of the work. Every part has a name and a job.

The parts and what they do

PFD ⭐ Personal Flotation Device — your life jacket. Coast Guard approved, fits snug, ZIPPED UP. EVERY ride. No exceptions."Life jacket first."
Bow The FRONT of the kayak. Pointy end goes first."Pointy end forward."
Stern The BACK of the kayak. Where the rudder lives."Where you steer from."
Hull The body of the kayak. Sits ON the water. Strong plastic."The skin of the kayak."
Seat Adjustable. Should be snug. Back support keeps your spine happy on long rides."Snug seat."
Pedals Like bike pedals. PUSH with your feet in cycles. The pedals turn the fins under the kayak."Push and pedal."
Pedal drive (fins) Under the kayak. Wag back and forth like a fish's tail. THIS is what pushes you forward."Like a fish tail."
Rudder A small fin under the back. Hand control turns it left or right. Steers WITHOUT slowing you down."Hand controls direction."
Paddle The backup. Use it when you start, when you stop, in shallow water, or as a brake. Always keep it ON the kayak."Backup. Always there."
Bow handle A strap or handle at the front for carrying."For carrying."

Ocean kayaking has clear rules — most of them about safety. The ocean is bigger and stronger than you. The rules keep you safe so you can keep paddling for the rest of your life.
⭐

1. PFD on, every time

Personal Flotation Device. Coast Guard-approved. Zipped up. Snug enough that you can't pull it over your head. EVERY time you get in the kayak. Even at the dock.

"PFD first. Always."
🌤️

2. Check the weather BEFORE you go

Wind forecast (under 10 mph for kids). Wave forecast (under 1 foot). Storm warnings (no go). Marine forecast — not just regular weather.

"Marine forecast first."
🗺️

3. Tell someone where you're going

Before you launch: tell someone on land where you'll go and when you'll be back. This is called a "float plan."

"Float plan to a person."
👀

4. Stay near the shore

At your stage, ALWAYS stay within sight of land. Most rides keep the shore within a few hundred yards. The ocean is BIG.

"Land always in sight."
⏰

5. Watch the tide

Tides go IN and OUT every 6 hours. Paddling AGAINST a strong outgoing tide is tiring. Plan to leave WITH the tide and return WITH the tide.

"Go with the tide."
🚤

6. Watch for boats

Bigger boats can't see you easily. Stay out of channel markers. Look BOTH ways before crossing a channel — like crossing a street.

"Look both ways."
🥶

7. Cold water = real danger

In Massachusetts, ocean water is COLD until July (and after September). Cold water can shock the body fast. Wear a wetsuit or stay out when it's cold.

"Cold = real risk."
🛟

8. If you tip — STAY WITH THE KAYAK

If you fall out, DO NOT swim away. Hold on to the kayak. It floats. The kayak is your best safety device. Wait for help.

"Stay with the kayak."
📞

9. Carry a way to call for help

Phone in a waterproof case. Or a whistle (3 short blasts = SOS). Best to have both.

"Whistle + phone."
🤝

10. Be a great paddler

Wave to other kayakers. Help if someone needs it. Don't paddle close to people's boats or docks. Leave no trash.

"Wave. Help. Leave it clean."
The ocean does not care if you're tired. The rules are not to slow you down — they're what let you come back to the water every summer, for the rest of your life.
Every move in a kayak has a name and a feel. Some you do with your feet (pedaling), some with your hands (rudder, paddle), some with your whole body (sitting balanced).

The 6 essential moves

🦵

Pedaling forward

Push DOWN on each pedal in alternation, like a bike. Smooth even circles. Don't stomp. Let your legs do the work — not your back.

"Smooth. Even. Steady."
↩️

Backing up

Most pedal drives reverse — pedal BACKWARDS. Or use the paddle: short reverse strokes at the bow.

"Reverse the pedal."
🎛️

Steering with rudder

The hand control turns the rudder. Push the control LEFT to go LEFT. RIGHT to go RIGHT. Gentle moves. Don't over-steer.

"Gentle hand."
🛑

Stopping

Stop pedaling. The kayak will glide. To stop FASTER: use the paddle as a brake (hold it in the water alongside the kayak).

"Glide. Or paddle-brake."
⚖️

Sitting balanced

Sit UPRIGHT, not slouched. Center your weight. If the kayak rocks, MOVE WITH IT — don't fight it. Loose hips, calm breath.

"Loose hips. Calm breath."
🚣

Paddle stroke (backup)

Hold the paddle with hands shoulder-width apart. Reach forward, plant the blade in the water, PULL back along the kayak. Switch sides.

"Reach, plant, pull."

The PFD Check (every ride)

The 4-Point check:

  • Zipped — fully zipped to the top.
  • Buckled — all clips snapped.
  • Snug — can't pull it over your head when lifted by the shoulders.
  • Fit — Coast Guard size matches your body weight (label on the inside).
"Zipped. Buckled. Snug. Fit."

Getting In and Out (the safe way)

From a beach or dock — the brace method:

  • Kayak in the water, floating, pointed parallel to shore.
  • ONE hand on the seat, ONE on the dock (or sand).
  • Step into the COCKPIT first with the foot closest to the kayak.
  • Lower butt down to the seat SLOWLY.
  • Bring the other foot in. Adjust pedals. Snug PFD. Go.
"Brace. Step. Sit. Adjust."

If You Tip Over (the wet exit)

If the kayak flips — DON'T PANIC:

  • Your PFD will bring you to the surface. Breathe.
  • Hold on to the kayak — DO NOT swim away from it.
  • If close to shore: swim the kayak to shore.
  • If far from shore: stay with the kayak, blow your whistle (3 short blasts), wait for help.
  • If you can, try to flip the kayak upright. Climb back in from the SIDE, bracing with both hands.
"Stay calm. Stay with the kayak."
The structure stays the same so you can predict what's coming. The content inside the structure changes so it never gets boring. Four trip formats roll in over the year — starting with land only for the first 6 weeks. Each scales to a 60-min trip with Coach or a 30-min practice with Daddy. BYC Junior Kayaking runs in the summer — the rest of the year, the land formats keep you sharp.

Why formats, not single-skill weeks

Kayaking has SO many parts. Boat parts. Knots. Wind directions. Maneuvers. If we tried to learn one thing per week, you'd forget the first by the time we got to the fifth. Instead, every practice follows the same shape: warm up → boat check → new skill → practice → game → pack up. The shape is the constant. What goes inside each block is what evolves. Every part you learn this week shows up next week in a different combination — it never disappears.

Built-in transitions (PFD check, water break, knot drill on the dock) are part of the practice, not interruptions. They reset attention between blocks. Plan for them.

The Four Formats

One format starts. A second is added at week 7. A third at week 27. A fourth at week 40. After each addition, formats rotate — never converge.

"Look at this. Let's name it."

The Warm-Up Menu

These are the foundation moves. They never go away. They live in the warm-up of every format, every session — picking 1–3 each time, rotating through. New foundation moves get added; old ones fade and return.

⭐ PFD Check

Zipped. Buckled. Snug. Right size. Every ride.

"Zipped. Buckled. Snug."

🌤️ Marine Forecast Check

Wind under 10 mph. Waves under 1 foot. No storms in the next 3 hours. If not — postpone.

"Marine forecast first."

🤸 Body Wake-Up

Arm circles, leg swings, hip openers, ankle circles. 30 seconds each.

"Wake the body."

🛶 Kayak Check

Pedals smooth? Rudder moves left and right? Paddle clipped on? Plug in?

"Pedals. Rudder. Paddle. Plug."

🚀 Dock Launch Drill

Practice the brace-step-sit-adjust sequence ON LAND first. Then in shallow water.

"Brace. Step. Sit. Adjust."

🦵 Slow Pedal

Pedal at half speed. Smooth circles. Both feet doing equal work.

"Smooth. Equal."

↩️ Rudder Practice

While pedaling slow, gently push the rudder control left. Then right. Notice how the kayak turns.

"Gentle hand."

🐟 Spot Something

Look for one thing in the water: a fish, a seagull, a piece of seaweed. Builds eyes-up habit.

"Eyes up. See it."

🛑 Stop Practice

Pedal forward. Daddy says "STOP!" Stop pedaling. Glide. Then dip the paddle in as a brake.

"Stop. Glide. Brake."

📍 Look Around

Slowly turn your head all the way left. All the way right. Look behind. Builds situational awareness.

"360 view."

The 52-Week Plan

A year of kayaking. The format rotates. The skills build. Each week has a focus and a video. Skip ahead if a piece clicks fast. Repeat any week as needed. BYC Junior Kayaking happens in summer — phases 2–4 are designed around that. The off-season is for knots, dry-getting ready, and watching the wind in your own backyard.

Phase 1 · Discover the Kayak

Format A only. No water yet. Every part, every knot, every wind direction — on land.

Week 1A

Discover the Kayak

Touch each part of the kayak: bow, stern, hull, seat, pedals, rudder, paddle. Name them out loud.

Week 2A

PFD Fitting

The 4-point PFD check. Zipped, buckled, snug, right size. Practice until it's second nature.

Week 3A

Getting In & Out

The brace-step-sit-adjust sequence. On dry land first. Then very shallow water with Daddy holding the kayak.

Week 4A

Discover Pedaling

Smooth even circles with the feet. In a sheltered cove. 50 yards straight in calm water.

Week 5A

Discover Steering

The rudder control — gentle moves. Turn in a big lazy circle. Then a smaller one.

Week 6A

Discover Stopping

Stop pedaling, glide. Then paddle-brake. Aim to stop at a buoy.

Phase 2 · Adding Paddling Skills

Formats A & B alternate. Getting the kayak ready. Paddling away from the dock. The first real trips on the water.

Week 7A

Backing Up

Reverse pedaling. Slow and controlled. Practice backing into a dock.

Week 8B

Dock Approach & Departure

Pull up to a dock, hold position, push off smoothly. Repeat.

Week 9A

Paddle Stroke

Forward paddle stroke. Reach, plant, pull. Hands shoulder-width.

Week 10B

Pedal + Paddle Combo

Pedal AND paddle for extra speed. Then paddle alone in shallow water.

Week 11A

Sweep Strokes

A wide arcing paddle stroke to turn. Useful when the rudder isn't enough.

Week 12B

Turn Race

Two buoys. Pedal around one, sweep-stroke around the other. Time it.

Week 13A

Reading Wind

Wind blows your kayak around. Notice which way. Pedal slightly INTO the wind.

Week 14B

Wind Drill

Daddy calls wind direction. Paddle on different headings. Notice how each feels.

Week 15A

Reading Waves

Waves come from a direction. POINT INTO waves. Sideways = unstable.

Week 16B

Wave Practice

In light chop, point the bow at oncoming waves. Notice the stability.

Week 17A

Self-Rescue Basics

If you tip, stay with the kayak. Practice "hold and signal" in shallow water with Daddy.

Week 18B

Whistle Drill

3 short blasts = SOS. Practice. Make sure whistle is clipped to the PFD.

Week 19A

Reading Tides

Outgoing = water flows out. Incoming = in. Plan around them.

Week 20B

Tide Walk

Look at a tide chart together. Pick an incoming-tide window. Then go.

Week 21A

Channel Crossing

Stop. Look both ways. Cross at 90°. Don't dawdle.

Week 22B

Channel Drill

Approach a real channel with Daddy. Practice the look-both-ways routine.

Week 23A

Long-Distance Pace

A smooth efficient pace you can hold for an hour. Not too fast.

Week 24B

One-Hour Ride

First hour-long paddle. Snack and water at the halfway mark.

Week 25A

Landing on a Beach

Approach with bow forward, slow down, ride the last wave in.

Week 26B

Sippican Harbor Tour

End-of-phase celebration. Full harbor tour. Land at a beach for lunch.

Formats A, B, C rotate. Jibing for real. Kayaking on every point. Tip over is normal now.

First Real Trip

Format C debuts. Plan a route. Pack snacks. Float plan. Go.

Week 28A

Reading the Sky

Dark clouds = potential storm. Wind picking up = head back. Trust your gut.

Weather Awareness

Buoy-to-Buoy Trip

Anchoring

Cove Hopping

Drills get the body strong. Games get the spirit strong. Mix them in every paddle. Some work great solo. Some work better with Daddy nearby. Some are perfect when Alexander joins.
🚩

Buoy-to-Buoy Race CLASSIC

The Sippican Harbor classic. Pick 3 buoys in a triangle. Pedal to each in order, touch (don't crash), pedal to the next. Time it. Beat your time next week.

Why it's perfect for the brothers: built-in regulation (each buoy is a rest point), no winner-vs-loser (you race the clock), Daddy can be the timer in a chase kayak, and Alexander can pick the triangle one week, Nicolas the next.

"Buoy. Touch. Next."
🐢

Slow Race

LAST to finish wins. Pedal as slowly as you can without stopping. Builds balance and patience.

"Slower is harder."
🚧

Paddle Race

No pedals. Just paddle. 50 yards. Builds the backup skill so it's ready when you need it.

"Reach, plant, pull."
🎯

Rudder Slalom

Set up 5 floating markers (water balloons, lobster buoys). Weave through them using only the rudder. No paddle.

"Gentle hand. Smooth turns."
🔄

Cove-to-Cove Relay

Pick 2 small beaches. Pedal from one to the other. Touch the shore. Come back. Time it.

"Touch the sand."
🔍

Treasure Hunt

Daddy makes a list ("a red buoy, a sea bird, a jellyfish, a moored sailboat"). Pedal around. Find them all.

"Eyes wide. Find them."
👂

Echo Game

Quiet on the water. Listen for one minute. Name every sound: wind, water, boats, birds. Calming.

"Stop. Listen."
🎣

Fishing from the Kayak

Bring a simple rod. Pedal to a quiet spot. Drop a line. The goal is just being out there with a line in the water.

"Quiet hands. Patient line."
⛵

Boat Spotter

Pedal around the harbor. How many different boat types can you name? Sailboat, motorboat, kayak, paddleboard, lobster boat, jet ski.

"Name what you see."
🌅

Sunrise / Sunset Quiet Paddle

No game. No racing. Just paddle slowly in the magic light. Talk OR be quiet. Both are fine.

"Slow. Look around."
🚤

Wake Riding (safe)

When a motorboat goes by FAR away, the waves reach you. Point INTO them. Let the kayak rise and fall. Fun.

"Bow into the wave."
🧭

Compass Practice

Daddy says "Pedal north!" Then "Pedal east!" Use the sun or a compass to find each direction.

"Find north first."

Make any paddle more fun

  • Add a story. "You're exploring an island no one has visited. Map it."
  • Add silly cues. "Pedal like a sea turtle. Smooth. Steady. Wise."
  • Add a target. A specific beach, a specific buoy. A goal makes the paddle a journey.
  • Add a snack stop. Mid-paddle beach landing. Apples taste better on a beach you paddled to.
  • Add a name. Give the kayak a name. "She" or "he" — the kayak has a personality.
  • Add music or quiet. Some days music. Some days just water and wind. Both are perfect.

🌊 Local launches near Marion, MA

Marion is one of the best places in New England for ocean pedal kayaking. Some of the best launches:

  • Sippican Harbor (Marion) — your home water. Tawny Town Beach, Silvershell Beach, and the town launch all give access to a sheltered harbor that's perfect for early kayaking. Calm in the mornings.
  • Silvershell Beach launch — kid-friendly. Sand bottom for easy launching. Lots of shoreline to land on.
  • Mattapoisett Harbor — slightly more exposed than Sippican. A good "next-level" harbor when ready.
  • Inner Buzzards Bay — when ready for more open water. Stay close to shore. Watch the wind forecast.
  • Cape Cod Canal access (more advanced) — strong currents here. NOT for the early stages. Save for when truly experienced.
  • Westport River / Cuttyhunk area — for big-day trips when much more advanced.

Critical: always check the marine forecast at weather.gov/marine before launching. Wind under 10 knots and waves under 1 foot for the early stages.

🛒 Pedal kayak brands and resources

  • Hobie — the gold standard for pedal kayaks. The MirageDrive is excellent. Models like the Pro Angler series, Outback, and Compass. Pricey but durable.
  • Old Town Sportsman PDL — slightly more affordable; great for fishing and family use.
  • Native Watercraft Slayer Propel — the Propel drive is similar to a bicycle (true forward and reverse).
  • Local dealers: Eastern Mountain Sports (Mansfield), Charles River Canoe & Kayak (rents and demos), Plymouth Bay Kayak (if open).
  • Buzzards Bay Coalition — savebuzzardsbay.org. Conservation org with paddling resources and group paddles for the bay.

🏃 Integrating Sarah PT with kayaking

Ocean pedal kayaking is a great pairing with Sarah's work. Specific overlaps:

  • Core stability for paddling. Every paddle stroke engages obliques and lower back. Sarah's core work translates directly. Ask her to add rotational core movements.
  • Leg power for pedaling. The pedal drive uses the same muscles as cycling. Single-leg push work, hip flexor strength, quad endurance — all Sarah territory.
  • Breathing during effort. Ocean kayaking is steady-state and self-regulated — even better than biking for the respiratory profile. You can stop and float anytime. Pair Sarah's breath work with long pedal stretches.
  • Upper body strength for paddle backup. When the paddle comes out, shoulder and lat strength matter. Sarah's overhead and pulling work pays off.
  • Recovery between trips. Kayaking is tiring in a deep way — sun, salt, water, effort. Sarah's recovery and stretch work matters. Plan rest days.
  • Balance and proprioception. Sitting balanced in a moving boat is a balance challenge. Sarah's wobble-board and single-leg work translates here.

Bonus framing: Sarah's PT work helps Nicolas paddle longer, recover faster, and stay comfortable on the water. Pairs the medical work with something he wants to be good at.

🛟 Equipment for ocean pedal kayaking with kids

  • PFD (Life Jacket). The MOST important piece. Must be Coast Guard-approved Type III. Must fit. Replace as the kid grows. Brands: NRS, Stohlquist, MTI, Astral.
  • Whistle. Required by Coast Guard. Clip it to the PFD. 3 short blasts = SOS.
  • Paddle. Even on a pedal kayak. Backup is critical. Wooden or carbon. Properly sized — the kid should be able to grip it with elbows at 90°.
  • Marine VHF radio OR phone in a waterproof case. A way to call for help. VHF channel 16 is the universal distress channel.
  • Sun protection. Long-sleeve UPF shirt, hat with chin strap, sunglasses (with a leash), reef-safe sunscreen. The sun on the water is brutal.
  • Water shoes or sandals with heel straps. Crocs, Tevas, Keens. Closed-toe protects from rocks; closed-back keeps them on.
  • Dry bag. For phone, snacks, change of clothes. Mandatory for anything you can't afford to lose.
  • Bilge pump / sponge. Pedal kayaks can take on water. A simple bilge pump or sponge for bailing.
  • Tow line. 15-foot floating line with a clip. For towing a friend or being towed.

🧠 Nicolas-Specific Suggestions

  • Pre-paddle routine. Same words, same order. "PFD. Whistle. Paddle. Water. Sunscreen. Float plan. Go." Predictability lowers transition anxiety.
  • Kayaking is GOOD for the respiratory profile. Steady-state. Self-paced. You can stop and float anytime. Pair Sarah's breath work with long pedal stretches.
  • Start very short. 20 minutes for the first trips. Build to longer. Trips that end while it's still fun = next time he'll want to go.
  • Snacks on the water. A familiar safe snack mid-paddle. Apples, crackers, water. The mid-paddle snack stop is a great regulation tool.
  • Visual schedule on the kayak bag. Same as other sports: velcro pictures.
  • Tandem kayak first. Daddy in the back, Nicolas up front. Reduces anxiety. Lets him build skills with safety.
  • Sippican Harbor in the morning. Calm, predictable, sheltered. Perfect first water.
  • End every paddle on a beach. A small landing, a moment, a treat. The "after the paddle" makes the paddle.
  • Watch boats together first. Sit on the dock. Watch other kayakers come in and out. Learn by watching before doing.
  • Bath protocol after a long paddle. Salt water + sun + effort = sore. The Epsom salt bath helps recovery.
Ocean kayaking is not just a sport. It's a moving meditation in self-regulation, motor planning, breath work, situational awareness, and joyful effort. A skill that opens up the entire coastline — every harbor, every cove, every summer evening. Every paddle is a developmental gift.
